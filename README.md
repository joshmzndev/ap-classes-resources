## II. How will we measure student learning?

### Grading Philosophy and Policy

* The majority of the assessments in this class will be formative and ungraded.  
* There will be 4 or more "unit tests" which will make up around 70% of the grade.  
* These unit tests will try to accurately measure a student's mastery of the content.  
* These unit tests can be retaken as many times as needed by the students, as long as they submit a **retake ticket**.  
* The unit tests will have different questions for each retake, but will test the same concepts.  
* At the end of each quarter, there will be a summative exam worth around 30% of the grade that **cannot** be retaken. This is to mimic the pressure of the real AP Exams.  
* The coverage of the summative exam will simply be the topics covered by the unit tests.  
* After the summative exam, the students will have **one (1)** chance to increase their summative exam grade by defending their mistakes in an oral test.   
* ***Rationale:*** Students learn at different rates, the tests should measure current mastery of the material and not how fast they learn it. A student who is able to master a concept by the end of a quarter should have the same grade as a student who is able to master it during the first week.

### Unit Test Format

Unit tests contain standard AP exam questions and custom questions authored by the instructor. Custom questions are usually free-response questions that are geared towards challenging misconceptions that are noticed during class or formative assessments.

Each unit test will always have 1-2 particularly difficult questions to challenge advanced students, but the perfect score will always be below the total points available.

### Unit Test Flow

All of these are automated and hosted through **akads.org**.

**Session One:** Students will take the unit test with closed notes. No devices allowed. The MCQs are checked automatically, and the free response will be automatically checked by AI using a 2-3 part rubric (1 point per criterion).

**Session Two:** Students will peer-check each other's answers, with priority given to matching students who have correct answers with answers marked "wrong" by AI - this is to increase the chance of correct checking. They will be given an anonymized free response answer along with the rubric, and will be asked to check the best way they can. Incentives are given to students who are able to accurately check their peer's answers.

*Rationale:* This will help students understand what the AP exam is asking for and solidifies their own knowledge by correcting or reasoning out wrong answers.

**Session Three:** The instructor will check answers in which students and the AI checking disagrees with each other, as well as spot checking answers that were marked fully correct to ensure academic integrity. The instructor's checking will be considered the final mark. Feedback and comments for each answer will be given here. 

**Session Four:** Students will be given their answers and a complete/revise verdict for each question. This is their chance to double check the AI's and their peer's checking and justify to the instructor that their free response answer is correct. They will have to fill in a retake ticket (a written reflection on the reasoning behind their incorrect answers) if they want to retake the test in the future. They are allowed to consult with their classmates, but they are not allowed to use any devices at all.

**Session Five:** Instructor will go through common misconceptions through Peer Instruction MCQ questions.

After **Session Four,** students can freely retake that unit test as long as they filled in their retake ticket.

### Leaderboard Points

For the formative assessments, there is a point system to keep track of students who show mastery of the concepts through other means. In the event that a student shows exceptional mastery through hands-on activities or socratic questioning but falls short in the AP-style unit tests, they will be considered for a higher grade.

* Points can be accumulated by demonstrating and presenting exceptional work, through oral defense or socratic questioning.  
* Written work or submitted work alone won't earn points.  
* These points will be tracked on a leaderboard. Your name and points can be kept private if you prefer.  
* By default, leaderboard points will add extra full points to your overall grade. This will depend on how much higher your points are compared to your peers.  
* Top placers in the leaderboard get to pick the next activity for the class and get first pick in groupings or pairings.

Examples of how to earn points in the leaderboard:

1. Reporting a vulnerability or flaw in a system that was not explicitly mentioned  
2. Discovering a quirk in some data that no one else found  
3. Showing full reasoning of how they solved a problem (even if the answer is inaccurate)  
4. Presenting an out-of-class discovery or achievement (ex. doing a picoCTF and being able to explain each step)

## III. How will the learning take place?

### Class Templates

Before each class, they will have "prep work" which is a 5-minute video or a short article to watch to expose themselves to the new content.

Each class will follow one of the following formats, depending on current progress and the result of the previous session.

**#1 Socratic Dialogue**  
- A concept or scenario is introduced and explained.
- Prior knowledge is activated (if applicable).
- Students are asked to make creative wrong answers based on their understanding.
- Students attempt to choose the correct answer among the creative wrong answers.
- Students are asked to write down the actual correct answer in their own words.

*Rationale:* students are most engaged and listen more attentively if it reduces the chance of them picking a wrong answer that their classmates have made. Top students who already quickly understand the material get to be more creative and are less bored.

**#2 Peer Instruction Day**  
- Students are given a conceptual question with multiple convincing answers.
- They vote once anonymously. 
- After voting, if the class is split (30% to 70%), they are asked to discuss among themselves - native language allowed.
- They vote again.
- Instructor discusses and corrects misconceptions as needed.

*Rationale:* Having students discuss amongst themselves is a proven method of learning efficiently. Students are forced to justify their answers and they often understand better if it is worded by a peer or if it is said in their native language.

**#3 Hands-on Demonstration**  
- Instructor goes through a tool or a real-world file and explains.
- Frequently, students are asked to provide a short answer for every step.
- After the demonstration is over, students will be asked to do it themselves while instructor circulates.

**#4 Test Day**  
- The whole period will be dedicated to taking a test through **akads.org**.
- Future sessions after a test day will be used to do peer checking and reviewing the test.

**#5 Review Day**  
- **akads.org** generates short-form questions based on their incorrect answers during tests.
- The whole period will be dedicated to them going through their tailored review questions, instructor will circulate to help clarify.
